## Medium and line

- Visibly hand-drawn ink combined with oil pastel/chalk.
- Clear, controlled, continuous ink contours drawn without a ruler.
- Lines may waver subtly, vary in pressure, overlap naturally and remain imperfect.
- Preserve human irregularity while keeping subjects and architecture legible.
- Never use dashed, dotted or technical construction lines.

## Detail and composition

- Reduce detail to what is needed for recognition and narrative meaning.
- Prefer decisive silhouettes and large areas over fussy rendering.
- Keep generous negative space for editorial typography.
- Historical architecture and essential objects must remain recognizable, even when simplified.
- Avoid perfect symmetry and machine-clean perspective.

## Colour and material

- Use large, bold, visibly worked oil-pastel/chalk fields.
- Palette: warm plaster white `#F1EBDD`, paper/ivory `#D8C9AF`, graphite `#201D1A`, aged gold `#A98235`, patina turquoise `#47716D`, grey-green `#70745A`, oxblood `#6B2F2B`, dusty rose `#B77D72`, plus the established dense ultramarine.
- Gold must feel tactile and aged, never like a flat yellow sun or a polished 3D material.
- Preserve the restraint of the opening; save the fullest ornamental intensity for the later Frieze climax.

## Never

- No photorealism or generic AI-polished rendering.
- No vector-clean edges, gradients or synthetic perfect surfaces.
- No paper-collage look unless explicitly requested.
- No invented historical details, text, objects, dialogue or plot.
- No arrows, generic icons, watermarks, dashed lines or decorative marks without narrative purpose.

## Continuity rule

For every new scene, treat both approved images as style references. Preserve the exact story function and historical content from the concept brief; vary only composition and emphasis required by that scene.
