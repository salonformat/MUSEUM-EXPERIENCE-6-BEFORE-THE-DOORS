# Sources, credits and usage notes

Last reviewed: 9 September 2026

This project is an original interactive interpretation by Salon Format. Historical sources are paraphrased and linked; the website does not reproduce the archival photographs or document scans on the referenced museum pages.

## Historical and curatorial sources

- Vienna Secession, [XIV Exhibition, 1902](https://secession.at/digital_archive_exhibition/56). Used for the dates, 58,000 visitors, Josef Hoffmann's direction, Max Klinger's *Beethoven* as the centrepiece, the wall opening and the exhibition's synthesis of architecture, painting and sculpture.
- Vienna Secession, [Beethoven Frieze](https://secession.at/beethovenfrieze). Used for the historical setting, the frieze's original position, its relation to Beethoven's Ninth Symphony and its present-day location.
- Vienna Secession Digital Archive, [Letter from the K.S. Hofkunsthandlung Ernst Arnold, 10 April 1902](https://secession.at/digital_archive/406), inventory I 1.4.4.-241. Used for the delayed marble head, the telegrams and the request for the price of Klinger's *Beethoven*.
- Vienna Secession Digital Archive, [Letter from Dr Gabriel von Térey, 12 April 1902](https://secession.at/digital_archive/1526), inventory I 5.3.5.-1538. Used for Térey's attendance, Joseph Beer's additional invitation and Hotel Kaiserhof.
- Vienna Secession, [Association of Visual Artists](https://secession.at/association_of_visual_artists_vienna_secession). Used for the statement that women artists appeared in some early exhibitions but women were not admitted as association members until 1949.

These sources are used for factual reference, short paraphrase and outbound links. Their linked photographs, scans and page design remain the property of their respective rights holders and are not republished here.

## Visual material

- The exterior, interior, spatial-model, correspondence and frieze images in `site/public/images/` are newly created illustrative reconstructions for this project. They are not archival document scans or museum photographs.
- Historical artworks and architecture visible in those reconstructions are interpreted in a reduced ink-and-pastel drawing style. No claim is made that the reconstructions are facsimiles.
- User-supplied reference screenshots were used as visual guidance only and are not distributed as source files by the public build.

Before commercial licensing or transfer to a client, Salon Format should retain the creation records for these illustrations and obtain written clearance from the Vienna Secession if the institution's name, archival material or building depiction is to be used in official partnership marketing.
