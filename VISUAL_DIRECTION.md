# Opening Experience — Visual and Interaction Direction

## Core idea

The opening should feel like entering an editorial illustration that slowly becomes spatial. It begins quiet, frontal and composed; crossing the threshold changes scale, light, sound and depth. The first interior moment then releases the visitor into a still-incomplete environment without turning it into a game or a conventional menu.

## Visual system

- The palette is rooted in Vienna around 1900 rather than reduced to “Klimt gold”: warm plaster white, paper ivory, graphite black, aged gold, muted turquoise, grey-green, oxblood and a restrained dusty rose.
- Opening palette, proposed digital values: Plaster `#F1EBDD`, Paper `#D8C9AF`, Graphite `#201D1A`, Aged Gold `#A98235`, Patina `#47716D`, Grey-Green `#70745A`, Oxblood `#6B2F2B`, Dusty Rose `#B77D72`.
- White and paper tones carry most of the opening. Graphite provides the Secession-era graphic contrast. Patina and grey-green establish exterior air, stone and shadow; oxblood and dusty rose appear only in small human or paper details.
- Gold is a controlled material event, not a glossy luxury wash. It begins as a narrow architectural or typographic signal and increases only as the experience moves toward the Frieze.
- This restraint protects the brief’s later visual escalation: the Frieze sequence can become materially richer, warmer and more ornamental than the surrounding interface.
- Flat planes, drawn architectural lines, paper grain and selective ornament instead of photorealistic reconstruction.
- Strong changes of scale: small editorial date lines against a monumental building silhouette; intimate handwritten marks against large quiet fields.
- Original Salon Format illustration placeholders are framed as production placeholders, never as faux-historical imagery.
- Salon Format handwriting is a signature layer for sparse human annotations, directional traces and selected emphasis. It will not carry essential long-form information. Until the original handwriting asset is supplied, use a clearly labelled placeholder rather than a generic script font.
- No use of the current Secession logo or brand identity.

## Typography

- Display: an elegant, high-contrast editorial serif for atmosphere and cultural weight.
- Information: a restrained grotesk/sans serif for dates, labels, accessibility controls and compact context.
- Salon Format handwriting: a limited third voice for human presence and tactile annotation.
- Type enters by line or phrase with generous pauses. Essential copy remains high-contrast, readable and available without motion.

## Movement

- Slow parallax between paper planes rather than simulated 3D camera spectacle.
- Slightly imperfect reveals: drawn lines settle, paper edges drift by a few pixels, light breathes subtly.
- Movement responds to scroll, pointer, keyboard and touch, but never tests speed or precision.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces parallax and depth travel with short dissolves and direct state changes.

## Sequence 01 — Exterior / date

The screen opens on near-black, then a warm paper field appears. A full-bleed placeholder for an original Salon Format illustration of the Secession exterior sits low and monumental, with generous sky above it. The building feels new and slightly strange, not picturesque.

The exact copy appears in three measured beats:

> VIENNA  
> 15 APRIL 1902

> The exhibition opens today.

> You should probably come inside.

The first two lines behave like an editorial dateline. The final sentence becomes the contextual invitation to enter. The doorway itself is the primary affordance; focus, hover or touch adds a restrained shift of light. No persistent “Next” button.

Sound, if enabled: sparse exterior air and restrained distant movement. No music is required. All meaning remains visible in text.

## Sequence 02 — Crossing the threshold

Activating the doorway does not cut immediately to another page. The exterior illustration separates into shallow paper layers while the doorway remains fixed. The frame darkens around it and the visitor moves through a compressed corridor of shadow. Exterior ambience narrows and falls away; a quieter interior room tone replaces it.

A brief plaster-to-graphite-to-paper tonal passage marks the architectural threshold. A thin aged-gold edge remains in view while muted patina falls away outside. Door edges and drawn lines can momentarily become the layout grid of the next scene, making the transition feel designed rather than cinematic in a generic way.

The transition lasts only long enough to register a change of place. It remains skippable by continued input and becomes a direct dissolve under reduced motion.

## Sequence 03 — First interior moment

The visitor arrives in a wide, calm, partially prepared room. The viewpoint is slightly off-centre to avoid a formal virtual-tour look. Large quiet surfaces and incomplete illustrated elements imply work still underway without inventing any specific undocumented action.

The exact opening copy appears with a short pause:

> You’re here. Good.

> There are still a few things to sort out.

After the copy settles, three points of attention emerge organically in the environment:

- CORRESPONDENCE
- THE ROOMS
- THE FRIEZE

They are spatial cues, not dashboard cards. Each begins as a faint typographic or drawn annotation tied to a placeholder object or architectural zone. The visitor may look around and discover them in any order; this phase establishes their presence but does not yet build the later acts.

Salon Format handwriting appears here for the first time as a subtle human trace near one point of attention, signalling that the building is being prepared by people without inventing dialogue.

Sound, if enabled: restrained room tone, a distant footstep or paper movement, followed by useful silence. Captions or a compact transcript control accompany any meaningful sound cue.

## Interface and accessibility

- Minimal controls sit at the edge of the composition: sound, language-ready structure and accessibility.
- Keyboard focus is visible and visually integrated.
- Touch targets remain generous without appearing as standard app buttons.
- Essential text is selectable, scalable and never baked into illustration.
- Contrast, sound-off comprehension and reduced-motion behavior are treated as part of the visual direction from the first prototype.

## Required original assets for the next phase

- Salon Format exterior illustration of the Vienna Secession in 1902.
- Salon Format interior illustration layers or approved art direction for constructing them.
- Original Salon Format handwriting samples or a handwriting asset with usage permission.
- Paper textures, line work and ornament elements made or approved by Salon Format.
- Any audio must be original or appropriately licensed.

## Deliberate exclusions in this phase

- No correspondence document interaction.
- No spatial-model manipulation.
- No Beethoven Frieze sequence.
- No invented historical details, dialogue, crowd scene or crisis.
- No generic AI-generated historical imagery.

## Colour research basis

- The Vienna Secession describes the Beethoven Frieze as a decisive move toward two-dimensional depiction, expressive line and dominant ornament, marking the beginning of Klimt’s golden period.
- The Belvedere records the Frieze’s material range as casein colour, stucco, drawing, glass, mother-of-pearl and gold applications. Gold therefore behaves as material and surface, not merely as a flat brand colour.
- Wien Museum research on Viennese poster art around 1900 identifies hard black-and-white contrasts, unconventional colour adjacencies, geometric ornament and large colour fields; a contemporary description of an 1899 Secession poster specifically evokes blue and yellow.
- The Leopold Museum’s *Attersee* (1900), first shown at the Secession in 1901, supports turquoise with grey and green as a period-appropriate counterweight to gold.
